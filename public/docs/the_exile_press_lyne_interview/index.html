The People We Were Told Were Never Real

An eight-month investigation found evidence that Estelorian folklore was describing something real all along - and that a state agency most citizens have never heard of has been hunting those people for five hundred years.

MD Maren D. · Investigations · 18 October 2022 9 min read 18
Share Save
Note on sourcing. Several names and identifying details in this piece have been changed or withheld to protect people whose safety could be compromised. Commenters appear under automatically assigned pseudonyms. We do not publish information that could be used to locate or identify anyone at risk.

Most people in Esteloria will tell you the old peoples never existed at all. The elves of the ballads, the kitsune (or gumiho), the cat-eared folk and the other creatures that fill the country's folklore - not lost, not extinct, but invented. Bedtime stories. You grow up hearing them, and then you're taught they were never anything more than that.

We spent eight months trying to verify that assumption. We couldn't.

Instead, we found evidence that people matching the descriptions from those old accounts have survived into the modern day - not as isolated individuals, but as communities, with families and children, hidden across the country and beyond its borders. The number of survivors is far larger than the word "survivors" tends to imply.

We also found something most citizens have never heard of. An agency called TPF.

TPF stands for The Purifying Flame. Its origins go back to 1482, when King Edmund III issued his first royal decree founding an order to hunt, prosecute, and execute non-human peoples throughout Esteloria. Over the centuries the name changed and the structure was modernised, but the mission didn't stop. In 1934, under the republic, the Flame was reorganised as the TPF Research and Detainment Division - a state agency with authority over nearly every other government body, shielded by confidentiality laws that keep its operations out of public view.

It is still operating.

We found evidence of an organization working against it. They call themselves Vaelthorn Network. By the accounts we have been able to gather, they have been extracting people from TPF facilities and concealing them for years - long enough that TPF itself apparently did not learn Vaelthorn's name until 2013, following a facility breakout that left dozens of TPF personnel dead.

We were able to arrange an interview with a woman who calls herself Lyne, believed to be one of Vaelthorn's senior operatives. She spoke to us over an audio-only call and would not confirm her location.

The interview
Full transcript · audio call

"They were about real people"

Edited for length and clarity. Lyne's exact location was not disclosed to the Press.

The Press
How should we refer to you?
Lyne
Lyne is fine. It's not my birth name, but it's what I go by.
The Press
You agreed to this interview. Why?
Lyne
Because we're at the point where it's time people knew. I've spent years watching this go completely unreported - not suppressed, not covered up. Just unreported. Nobody writes about it because nobody thinks there's anything to write about. As long as the assumption is that we're folklore, everything being done to us stays invisible.
The Press
So you want to change that assumption.
Lyne
I want to give people enough information to start asking their own questions. What they do after that is up to them.
The Press
Let's start with the basic claim. The elves, the kitsune, the cat-eared folk from the old tales - most people here don't think they died out. They think they were never real to begin with. You're saying they're alive today.
Lyne
They're real, and they've been alive this whole time. And it isn't just one kind of people - there are several, more than most outsiders would ever guess, and they've been living among you for generations. In your cities. Sending their children to your schools. The ones who can pass as human do. The ones who can't have to disappear entirely.
The Press
And this isn't a small number of people.
Lyne
No. I'm not going to give you a figure, but the word "handful" would be seriously wrong. These are communities. Families. Children growing up who've never known anything except hiding.
The Press
You mentioned TPF. For readers who've never heard the name - what is it, from where you stand?
Lyne
It's the thing our parents warned us about. The reason a family teaches its children to keep their ears covered before they teach them to read. On paper it's a government agency with a long history and a lot of legal cover. To us it's simpler than that: it's what comes for you if the wrong person ever finds out what you are. Everyone I've ever protected has lost someone to it.
The Press
What does it do now? In practice.
Lyne
Mostly, it finds people and kills them. That's the bulk of what TPF does, and it's what it was built to do - an extermination agency first, everything else second. Facilities and detainment happen too, when TPF wants someone alive for a reason. There's at least one program inside the agency built specifically around that. I'm not going to get into what it involves. We'll come back to that another time.
The Press
So detention isn't the norm.
Lyne
No. People assume TPF is mostly a research operation because the name has "research" in it. It isn't. Killing is the default. Being kept alive is the exception, and it's usually worse.
The Press
Tell us about Vaelthorn.
Lyne
We don't just wait until someone's already inside a facility and pull them out after the fact. We go after TPF directly - tracking their transports, hitting routes, going into facilities before intake happens, not only after. Extraction is what it looks like when prevention has already failed. And getting someone out is only half of it; keeping them safe afterward is often the harder job. That's the short version. The real work is planning, timing, and a lot of very dangerous nights.
The Press
How long has Vaelthorn been operating?
Lyne
Longer than TPF realises.
The Press
Does Vaelthorn use violence?
Lyne
Extractions are the flattering part of the story, the part that sounds justifiable. But I won't frame Vaelthorn as a rescue service that occasionally has to get its hands dirty. Our people have been hunted for five hundred years. We fight the ones doing the hunting - quietly, for a long time, because being seen would have gotten everyone killed. It isn't something we resort to when a rescue goes wrong. It's most of what we do. TPF only learned our name in 2013. That doesn't mean we weren't there before.
The Press
People have been killed in your operations.
Lyne
People have been killed in TPF operations for five centuries. People have died in ours too. I'm not going to pretend otherwise.
The Press
Some would call you a resistance movement. A paramilitary.
Lyne
Call it whatever makes sense to you. The labels matter less to me than the fact that the work exists and someone has to do it. If we stopped, there would be no one else. That's not a complaint. It's just the situation.
The Press
Why come forward now? Why 2022?
Lyne
For a long time, staying invisible was the only thing keeping people alive, so that's what we did. It worked - but it never changed anything. The hunting didn't stop. It just stayed hidden along with us. At some point you realise that hiding forever means the system outlives everyone in it, and nothing gets better for the next generation. People can't demand their government stop doing something they don't even know is being done. So I decided it was time at least one of us was willing to be seen and say it out loud. We'll keep protecting our people the same way we always have. But silence was never going to be enough on its own.
The Press
You want public pressure.
Lyne
I want people to have enough information to make up their own minds. That's it.
The Press
Aren't you worried that publishing this puts the people you protect in more danger?
Lyne
That's the thing I worry about most. It's why I'm not giving you names or locations, and why almost nothing in this piece is pictured. The one photograph we agreed to run took a long conversation before I said yes. But the alternative is staying invisible forever, and I've watched what that does. If nobody knows you exist, nobody fights for you. Nobody even thinks to ask where you went.
The Press
Are you afraid?
Lyne
I used to be. Not anymore.
The Press
Of being identified?
Lyne
TPF already knows who I am. They've known for years - my name, my face, that I run Vaelthorn. Knowing and catching are two different problems, and they've never been good at the second one. So this interview doesn't expose me; there's nothing left to expose. I still weighed every word, because plenty of people around me are not known, and I won't be the reason that changes. But for myself? No. That fear stopped being useful a long time ago.
The Press
Is there something you'd want to say directly to people reading this?
Lyne
I'm not angry at anyone for not knowing. Nobody told you. You grew up hearing the stories and then you were told to stop believing them, and you did, because that's what a reasonable person does. I'm asking you to reconsider. Not because the fairy tales got every detail right - but because they were about real people. And those people are still here, and they could use someone noticing.
The Press
Is there anything you'd add?
Lyne
No, I think you have enough to work with. I hope you print it.

Lyne declined to say where Vaelthorn operates, how many people belong to it, or how many extractions it has carried out. She did not identify any of the individuals whose existence we have been investigating independently.

She was more willing to discuss the system itself - the legal framework that gives TPF its authority, what she says happens inside its facilities, and why she believes public awareness will help to change it. Her account is consistent with documents provided through her intermediary, which were reviewed by two editors.

If nobody knows you exist, nobody fights for you. Nobody even thinks to ask where you went.
What we could verify

We reviewed documents and photographs provided through intermediaries connected to Lyne, alongside testimony from multiple independent sources. None of them individually proves the full history she described. Taken together, they point consistently in the same direction: people matching the old descriptions have survived into the present, TPF has been operating against them, and a network has been working to get them out.

Documents Internal TPF paperwork provided through sources, referencing "subjects" and classification numbers. Reviewed by two editors. Consistent
Photographs Several images provided by sources corroborate testimony but cannot be independently dated. Partial
Testimony Multiple sources describe overlapping details without prior contact with one another. Corroborated
Government The Ministry of Internal Security was approached for comment. No response

We are withholding several pieces of evidence because publishing them would make it possible to identify living people. This isn't a statement about their authenticity. It's a decision about what journalism should cost its sources.

We put the findings of this investigation to the Ministry of Internal Security. The Ministry did not respond. We were unable to contact TPF directly - the agency maintains no public-facing office and no published contact information that we could find.

This investigation will continue.
